## Explanation

Learning a practice is not acquiring knowledge in isolation but becoming a member of a community. Peripheral tasks are legitimate because they are part of the real work, so newcomers learn the practice and build relationships at the same time. As they learn, they take on more central tasks and come to see themselves as members, which sustains their participation. The theory describes a trajectory from periphery to core, so it is classified as a lifecycle process theory.

## Relevance to software engineering

Open source projects are communities of practice with visible peripheral work: reporting bugs, answering questions, and submitting small patches. The theory predicts that what happens after joining, rather than initial motivation, determines who stays.
