---
title: Does modular design make software less error-prone and easier to change?
summary: Low coupling and high cohesion are among the oldest design principles. Do modules that depend less on others and do one thing well really have fewer defects and cost less to change?
area: organization
---

## Why it matters

Architects trade modularity against performance, delivery speed, and simplicity. If coupling and cohesion predict defects and change effort, design metrics can steer reviews and refactoring; if not, the principles rest on intuition alone.

## Scope

Includes coupling, cohesion, and related structural properties of modules or classes, related to fault-proneness and change effort. Excludes the mirroring between organizations and architecture, covered by a separate question.
