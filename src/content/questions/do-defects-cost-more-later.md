---
title: Do defects cost more to fix the later they are found?
summary: A widely taught rule says a defect fixed after release costs many times more than one fixed during requirements or design. Does the cost of fixing really grow with the time a defect stays in a project?
area: quality
---

## Why it matters

The rule justifies heavy up-front investment in requirements, design reviews, and early verification. If the escalation is real and large, front-loading quality work pays off; if it is small or absent in modern processes, that investment should be weighed against cheaper later fixes.

## Scope

Includes the effort or cost to resolve defects as a function of the phase in which they are introduced and the phase in which they are found. Excludes the business cost of failures in the field, such as lost customers.
